# Stitched Light (조각보 빛)

*An algorithmic philosophy — a generative aesthetic movement*

## The Movement

Stitched Light is the computational aesthetics of the remnant: the belief that beauty is not designed whole, but accumulated from fragments joined with care. Its ancestor is the jogakbo — the Korean patchwork wrapping cloth sewn from leftover scraps of ramie and silk — hung in a window so that sunlight passes through every seam. In this movement, the canvas is never painted; it is *pieced*. Space is divided, and divided again, by a recursive process that behaves like a frugal seamstress: each cut is made where the cloth allows, each fragment kept, nothing discarded. The final surface must feel like it took years of quiet handwork — a meticulously crafted algorithm refined through countless iterations by someone at the absolute top of their field.

## Division as Composition

The generative core is recursive subdivision under constraint. A single bounded field splits along its longer axis, at proportions drawn from a small family of resonant ratios — near-halves and golden-section cuts — perturbed just enough that no seam feels machine-made. The probability of splitting decays with depth, so the field crystallizes into a population of patches: a few generous panels, many modest pieces, a scattering of tiny remnants tucked into corners. This distribution is the composition. There is no layout step, no arrangement pass; hierarchy and rhythm emerge purely from the statistics of the cut. The parameters that govern it — depth, split chance, seam width — are not decoration knobs but the temperament of the seamstress herself, and they must be tuned with the painstaking precision of master-level implementation.

## The Imperfect Hand

Every edge in Stitched Light is drawn twice: once by geometry, once by the hand. Vertices march along each seam and are displaced by coherent noise — a few pixels of wobble that turn rectangles into cloth. Stitch lines run along the seams in short dashes, slightly irregular, the visible record of labor. Some patches carry a faint diagonal crease, the ghost of having once been folded and stored — a fold remembered rather than shown. This is the movement's discipline: imperfection is never random scatter; it is *coherent* deviation, seeded and reproducible, so that the same cloth can be sewn again identically from the same number. The wobble amplitude, the dash cadence, the crease probability — each the product of deep computational expertise and painstaking optimization.

## Light Through Cloth

Color in this movement is not applied; it is *transmitted*. Each patch is a translucent membrane — persimmon, jade, indigo, and their ivory-washed tints — and behind the whole cloth drifts a single slow light source, like afternoon sun crossing a window. Patches nearer the light warm and brighten; patches at the far edge cool into shadow. Because the light moves and the cloth does not, the work breathes: the geometry is fixed at the moment of seeding, but its illumination is alive, evolving in slow harmonic drift. Sheerness is a first-class parameter — turn it up and the patches become gauze, seams glowing through; turn it down and the cloth becomes dense, saturated, opaque.

## Seeded Inevitability

Every cloth begins from a seed, and the seed is destiny: the same integer always sews the same bojagi, thread for thread. Adjacent seeds are siblings — recognizably of the same workshop, never identical. The viewer is invited not to draw but to *browse the workshop*: step through seeds, tune the seamstress's temperament, slide the sun across the sky. The algorithm must make every one of those thousands of unseen variations gallery-worthy, which is the true test of craft — not one lucky frame, but a distribution of beauty, held together by ratios and probabilities chosen through countless refining iterations. Beauty lives in the process; each run is one cloth from an infinite drawer of them.
